# health & safety

*Draft - pending legal review. Please read before playing.*

## photosensitive seizure advisory

A very small number of people may experience a seizure when exposed to certain
visual images, including flashing lights or patterns that may appear in video
games. Even people who have no history of seizures or epilepsy may have an
undiagnosed condition that can cause these "photosensitive epileptic seizures"
while watching video games.

These seizures may have a variety of symptoms, including:

- Lightheadedness, altered vision, eye or face twitching
- Jerking or shaking of arms or legs, disorientation, confusion
- Momentary loss of awareness or loss of consciousness
- Convulsions

**Stop playing immediately and consult a doctor** if you or anyone else
experiences any of these symptoms. Parents should watch for or ask their
children about these symptoms - children and teenagers are more likely than
adults to experience these seizures.

## reducing the risk

You can reduce the risk of photosensitive epileptic seizures by:

- Sitting farther from the screen
- Using a smaller screen
- Playing in a well-lit room
- Not playing when you are drowsy or fatigued

If you or any of your relatives have a history of seizures or epilepsy, consult
a doctor before playing.

## O.R.B.S. specifically

**Nothing in O.R.B.S. flashes.** This is a design decision rather than an
accident of the art style, and it is recorded as such: the boot sequence
originally opened with a screen flash and a bright band sweeping down the tube,
and both were cut. The screen now opens black and the prompt types itself.

The game is a text terminal drawn on a simulated CRT. There are no particle
effects, no explosions, no combat flashes, and no full-screen colour changes.
The brightest thing that happens is a character cell changing colour.

Two effects involve motion or brightness change, and both are slow and local:

- **The athanor's flame** and the **balneum mariae's roil** are animated colour
  ramps inside a single instrument's bar on the panel - a few character cells,
  cycling at a low rate.
- **Phosphor persistence and scanlines** in the CRT effect produce a faint,
  steady glow. They do not pulse.

### Motion sensitivity and the CRT

O.R.B.S. draws its terminal through a simulated CRT with **barrel distortion**
(a gentle curve to the screen), a vignette, and scanlines. If curved-screen
distortion causes you discomfort, **press `F3` to turn the CRT effect off
entirely.** The game is fully playable without it - the CRT carries no
information, only atmosphere.

`F2` cycles the phosphor colour scheme, which includes lower-contrast and
monochrome options.

> **Note for this build:** the `F3` and `F2` settings are **not yet saved
> between sessions.** Persisting them arrives with the settings screen. Until
> then you will need to press `F3` again each time you launch. If you are
> sensitive to this effect, that is worth knowing before you start.

## eye strain and fatigue

O.R.B.S. is a reading game. Text on a glowing background for long periods is
tiring, so this section matters more here than it would for most titles.

- Take a 10–15 minute break every 30–45 minutes of play
- Sit at a comfortable distance from the screen - about arm's length
- Adjust the brightness of your display so it is not too bright or too dim
- If the default phosphor is too bright, cycle it with `F2` or turn off the CRT
  glow with `F3`
- Avoid playing when you are tired or need sleep
- If your hands, wrists, or arms become tired or sore while playing, stop and
  rest for several hours before playing again

If persistent pain or discomfort continues, consult a doctor.

## repetitive strain

**O.R.B.S. is played entirely by typing.** There is no mouse input. This puts
the strain profile closer to a working day at a keyboard than to a typical game.

- Keep your wrists in a neutral position, not bent up or resting on a hard edge
- Use a keyboard and chair height that let your forearms stay roughly level
- Stretch your hands, wrists, and fingers regularly
- Stop playing at the first sign of pain, numbness, or tingling

**No part of the game requires fast typing.** The simulation advances once per
second and every command is queued to the next tick, so typing quickly earns you
nothing. Where a task would otherwise reward speed, the game's answer is to let
you automate it by writing a script - that is the central mechanic, not a
concession.

## stop playing and consult a physician if you experience

- Convulsions, eye or muscle twitching, loss of awareness
- Altered vision or involuntary movements
- Disorientation or dizziness
- Any pain, numbness, tingling, or weakness in your hands, wrists, arms, neck,
  shoulders, or back

## emergency

If someone experiences a seizure, keep them safe by moving nearby objects away,
do not restrain them, and seek medical help immediately.
